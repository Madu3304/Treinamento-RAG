## Abstract

Skeletal muscle atrophy is a serious health problem for astronauts in long-duration space flight under microgravity conditions. Preventative measures against muscle atrophy require specific exercise and dietary regimens. Preemptive measurements to improve treatment have the potential to streamline these regimens, decreasing their daily footprint and increasing astronaut quality of life. 

The objective of our proposed project is to:
1. Develop a fully integrated disposable microelectrode array chip (with the size of a stamp) that can be interfaced with a handheld electronic system for simultaneous detection of a panel of biomarkers to monitor the progression of skeletal muscle atrophy due to disuse under microgravity in long-duration spaceflights; and
2. Use such quantitative information to guide the combined countermeasures of physical exercise and pharmaceuticals (i.e., specific protease inhibitors) so that the intensity, duration, and frequency of exercise can be reduced.

## Schematic & Chip Overview

* **Microelectrode Array Chip:** Optical microscopy images showing the overall stamp-sized array ($1\text{ mm}$ scale bar) and a zoomed-in view of the $3 \times 3$ microelectrode pads ($500\ \mu\text{m}$ scale bar).
* **Sensing Mechanism:** Ferrocene ($\text{Fc}$)-tagged peptide substrates immobilized on a gold ($\text{Au}$) electrode surface facilitate **Electron Transfer**. Upon cleavage by a target **Protease**, the $\text{Fc}$ tags are released, resulting in **Decreased Electron Transfer** that can be quantitatively measured.
