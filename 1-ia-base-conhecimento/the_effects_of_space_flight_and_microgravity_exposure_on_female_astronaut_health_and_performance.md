## Space Flight as a Multifactorial Physiological Stressor

* Current understanding of space flight-induced impacts to physiology & performance:

  * Based on research in predominantly **MALE** populations.

  * Hormonal influence on numerous body systems can lead to sex-specific differences:

    * Cardiovascular Function

    * Bone Health

    * Energy Balance & Adiposity

    * Muscle Mass & Strength

    * Response to Physiological Stress & Energetic Status

## Delayed Inclusion of Females to NASA Astronaut Corps

* **"Mercury 13":**

  * Trained to be part of America's first human spaceflight program in the early 1960s.

  * Completed same intensive test battery used for male astronauts in the Mercury Program.

  * Delayed due to funding limitations and concerns for safety.

* **Key Milestones:**

  * **1963:** First female in Space — Valentina Tereshkova (Cosmonaut).

  * **1977:** First NASA astronaut class to include females.

  * **1983:** First U.S. female in space — Dr. Sally Ride, *Challenger* STS-7.

## Sex Differences in Space Flight-Related Research

* **Requires more research:**

  * Sex differences exist in numerous physiological systems.

  * Ignored/understudied for decades; males were considered an appropriate proxy for all humans.

* **NASA has sponsored workshops to address sex differences in space flight:**

  * *Sex, Space and Environmental Adaptation:* University of Missouri, 2002.

  * *NASA & NSBRI Virtual Workshop: The Impact of Sex & Gender on Adaptation to Space*, 2013.

  * *2014 J Women Health Vol 23 (11): The Impact of Sex and Gender on Adaptation to Space: A NASA Decadal Review*.

* **Artemis Lunar Exploration program increases diversity:**

  * First female astronaut to the Lunar surface by 2030.

  * $50\%$ female astronauts in Artemis-specific astronaut corps.

> **Key Takeaway:** Greater understanding of sex differences in space flight adaptations is critical to develop/improve countermeasures to minimize risk and maintain health in **ALL** astronauts.

## Methods

### 1. Demographic & Musculoskeletal (MSK) Injury/Diagnosis Data

* Queried from Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) — August 2022.

* Available data from Mercury through ISS Expedition 66 (Total: $360$ astronauts).

* MSK included muscle sprains/strains, tendonitis, ACL injury, fractures, diagnosis of osteopenia/osteoporosis:

  * Fracture data through May 2022.

  * Osteopenia/Osteoporosis through March 2019.

  * All other injuries through March 2020.

* Reported as In-Flight or Postflight ($R+0$ to $2$ years postflight).

### 2. Aerobic Capacity ($\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$) Characteristics

* Data from standardized medical assessments (MEDB):

  * **Preflight:** $L-3/1$ month.

  * **Postflight:** $R+3$ days.

* $\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$ testing on upright cycle ergometer (Lode Excalibur Sport), ramped protocol.

* Variables of interest: $\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$, peak watt.

* **Sample Size ($n = 47$):** $11$ Female, $36$ Male.

### 3. Leg Muscle Strength & Endurance Characteristics

* Data from MEDB:

  * **Preflight:** $L-3/1$ month.

  * **Postflight:** $R+5$ days.

* Isokinetic testing on dynamometer:

  * **Strength:** Knee extension @ $60^\circ/\text{sec}$.

  * **Endurance:** Knee extension @ $180^\circ/\text{sec}$.

* **Sample Size ($n = 87$):** $17$ Female, $70$ Male.

## Results: Musculoskeletal Injuries & Diagnoses

* **Total MSK injuries/diagnoses reported:**

  * $283$ injuries in $151$ astronauts.

  * $49.7\%$ had multiple injuries ($1.8$ injuries per astronaut, range $1\text{–}8$).

* **Overall Breakdown by Category:**

  * **Muscle Sprain/Strain:** $59\%$

  * **Tendon-related:** $20\%$

  * **Fracture:** $16\%$

  * **Low BMD:** $5\%$

* **Timing of Injuries:**

  * Postflight injuries were most common ($92\%$).

  * A large proportion of injuries ($49.6\%$) occurred within $1$ year of landing:

    * $7.3\%$ within $1$ month.

    * $8.1\%$ between $1\text{–}3$ months.

    * $9.2\%$ between $3\text{–}6$ months.

    * $25\%$ between $6\text{–}12$ months.

* **Sex Comparisons:**

  * **Females ($39$ injuries in $20$ astronauts):** Fewer total injuries reported, but higher prevalence ($1.95$ injuries per female); $20\%$ of female injuries occurred in-flight; Low BMD diagnoses in $15\%$ of females.

  * **Males ($244$ injuries in $131$ astronauts):** $1.86$ injuries per male; $4\%$ of injuries occurred in-flight; Low BMD diagnosed in $4\%$ of male astronauts.

## Results: Aerobic Capacity ($\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$)

### Preflight Crew Demographics (Aerobic Capacity Cohort)

| Metric | Female ($n = 11$) | Male ($n = 36$) | P-value | 
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **Age ($\text{yr}$)** | $44.5 \pm 6.6$ | $48.6 \pm 5.6$ | $0.041$ | 
| **Body Mass ($\text{kg}$)** | $68.8 \pm 10.0$ | $81.0 \pm 8.8$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Flight Duration ($\text{days}$)** | $205.0 \pm 58.3$ | $176.0 \pm 40.5$ | $0.061$ | 
| **$\text{VO}_{2\text{peak}}$ ($\text{ml/kg/min}$)** | $32.8 \pm 5.9$ | $37.8 \pm 6.0$ | $0.021$ | 
| **Peak Watt ($\text{W}$)** | $229.0 \pm 41.5$ | $299.0 \pm 52.4$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Peak Heart Rate ($\text{beats/min}$)** | $176.0 \pm 7.3$ | $173.0 \pm 10.8$ | $0.406$ | 

* **Preflight Differences:** Females were younger ($-4.2 \pm 2.0\text{ yrs}$), weighed less ($-12.2 \pm 3.1\text{ kg}$), had lower $\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$ ($-4.9 \pm 2.1\text{ ml/kg/min}$), and lower Peak Power ($-70.0 \pm 17.3\text{ W}$).

* **Postflight Changes ($\%\Delta$ pre-postflight):**

  * **A) Aerobic Capacity ($\text{L/min}$):** No significant sex difference (`ns`), with mean reductions of approximately $-11.5\%$ in females and $-9\%$ in males.

  * **B) Aerobic Power ($\text{W}$):** Statistically significant sex difference ($p < 0.01$, `**`), with females experiencing a greater reduction in peak power (mean $\sim -13.5\%$) compared to males (mean $\sim -5\%$).

## Results: Leg Muscle Strength & Endurance

### Preflight Crew Demographics (Strength Cohort)

| Metric | Female ($n = 17$) | Male ($n = 70$) | P-value | 
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **Age ($\text{yr}$)** | $42.8 \pm 3.7$ | $48.4 \pm 5.1$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Body Mass ($\text{kg}$)** | $65.7 \pm 6.3$ | $82.9 \pm 8.4$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Flight Duration ($\text{days}$)** | $179.0 \pm 50.3$ | $173.0 \pm 33.3$ | $0.478$ | 
| **Knee Extension Strength ($\text{Nm}$; $60^\circ/\text{sec}$)** | $150.0 \pm 24.6$ | $206.0 \pm 42.0$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Normalized Knee Extension Strength ($\text{Nm/kg}$; $60^\circ/\text{sec}$)** | $2.3 \pm 0.3$ | $2.5 \pm 0.5$ | $0.199$ | 
| **Knee Extension Endurance ($\text{Nm}$; $180^\circ/\text{sec}$)** | $1546 \pm 507$ | $2374 \pm 585$ | $< 0.001$ | 
| **Normalized Knee Extension Endurance ($\text{Nm/kg}$; $180^\circ/\text{sec}$)** | $23.7 \pm 6.4$ | $28.7 \pm 7.0$ | $0.019$ | 

* **Preflight Differences:** Females were younger ($-5.6 \pm 1.3\text{ yrs}$), weighed less ($-17.2 \pm 2.4\text{ kg}$), had lower absolute knee strength ($-56.1 \pm 10.6\text{ Nm}$) and lower absolute endurance ($-828 \pm 158\text{ Nm}$), though normalized knee strength was comparable to males.

* **Postflight Changes ($\%\Delta$ pre-postflight — Comparable Decrement Among All Astronauts):**

  * **A) Knee Extension Strength:** No significant sex difference (`ns`), with mean decrements of approximately $-17\%$ in females and $-10\%$ in males.

  * **B) Knee Extension Endurance:** No significant sex difference (`ns`), with mean decrements of approximately $-13.5\%$ in females and $-12\%$ in males.

## Summary

* Regardless of sex, space flight deconditioning could impact health and ability to complete EVA-associated tasks.

* Data indicates the majority of injuries occur postflight\*:

  * Implications for long-term health.

  * Potential for increased ($\uparrow$) in-flight injury prevalence in Artemis missions?

* Data indicates the majority of injuries occur in males (likely an artifact of fewer female astronauts).

* Females have a higher proportion of in-flight injuries\*.

* Females have a higher proportion of postflight low BMD diagnoses ($3\times$)\*.

* Comparable decrements in $\text{VO}_{2\text{pk}}$, knee extension muscle strength & endurance for all astronauts.

* Females showed a greater reduction in maximal external work (peak watts).

*\*Caveat — increased time frame postflight.*
